Part 1 — Concepts

What is ABS?

Asset-Backed Securities (ABS) is a way to convert a pool of future receivables into tradable securities today. Think of it as a company saying: "I have $10M coming in over the next 2 years — I'll package that future cash flow, sell it to investors now, and get capital immediately."

🥥 The Coconut Analogy Throughout this guide, we use a fruit supplier as a running example. The supplier has stable long-term contracts with hawker centers to deliver coconuts — those contracts represent predictable future cash flows, making them ideal ABS assets.

Key Parties in an ABS Transaction

🏭

1. Originator

Owns the receivables and wants cash now. Sells future income rights to the SPV. In Monee's case, Monee is the Originator (and also the Servicer).

🏛️

2. SPV

Special Purpose Vehicle — a separate legal entity created solely to hold the sold receivables and issue ABS notes to investors. Isolates risk from the Originator.

💰

3. Investor

Buys the ABS notes (usually Senior Notes). Provides upfront capital in exchange for scheduled interest and principal repayment backed by receivables.

🏪

4. Borrowers

Make scheduled payments generating the cash flow that backs the ABS. In the example: hawker centers paying their coconut contracts.

📊

5. Rating Agency

Independent third party evaluating credit risk of the ABS and assigning a rating. Adds credibility, like a mall endorsement for the hawker center.

⚙️

6. Servicer

Manages the loan portfolio on behalf of the SPV — collects repayments, monitors portfolio health, manages defaults. Monee acts as both Originator and Servicer.

SPV Deep Dive: Why Not Raise Money Directly?

For the Originator

  • Risk is separated — SPV is a different legal entity; transfers asset risk out, doesn't interrupt daily operations
  • The fruit supplier can keep running the shop while the SPV manages the coconut contract receivables
  • Bankruptcy remoteness: SPV assets are protected from Originator's creditors

For the Investor

  • Simpler and clearer — only exposed to a specific set of receivables (just the coconut business)
  • Don't need to worry about whether the fruit shop is also profitable at mangoes or durians
  • Senior Note holders get paid first — lowest risk tranche

Tranche Structure & Repayment Waterfall

ABS securities are split into tranches — slices of the asset pool with different risk/return profiles. This mechanism makes ABS attractive to a wide range of investors.

Senior Notes — ~70% · AAA/AA Rated · First Loss Protection
Junior Notes — ~30%

🔵 Senior Notes — Low Risk, Low Yield

  • Backed by the most stable, reliable loan receivables
  • Receive interest and principal before Junior Note holders in every payment cycle
  • Rated AAA/AA by rating agencies
  • Where most external investors participate
  • First priority in the repayment waterfall

🟠 Junior Notes — Higher Risk, Higher Yield

  • The first-loss tranche — if borrowers default, Junior noteholders absorb losses first
  • Earn excess spread — yield difference between portfolio interest income and senior coupon
  • Monee typically retains Junior Notes — signals confidence in loan quality and captures upside from strong portfolio performance

🌊 The Repayment Waterfall

When borrowers repay, cash flows into the SPV and is distributed in strict priority order — higher priority gets paid first, like water flowing downhill.

①
Transaction Costs & Trustee FeesRunning costs of the SPV structure
②
Servicer FeeCompensation to Monee for ongoing portfolio management
③
Senior Note Interest & PrincipalExternal investors paid first — lowest risk, AAA rated
④
Junior Note Interest & PrincipalMonee-retained tranche — absorbs losses, captures excess yield
⑤
Residual / Excess SpreadAny surplus after all payments → goes to Junior noteholder (Monee)
Why does Monee retain Junior Notes? By holding Junior Notes, Monee participates in the upside when portfolio performance is strong (excess spread income). It also signals confidence to investors — if Monee believes in its own loan quality, it keeps skin in the game. This is a feature, not a bug.

How Monee Benefits from ABS

Platform Benefits

  • Upfront liquidity — sell now, collect later; improves working capital for reinvestment
  • Risk isolation — clean transfer to SPV keeps balance sheet lean, even if loans underperform later
  • Retain upside — Monee retains Junior Notes, capturing any outperformance above expected losses
  • Repeatable & scalable — package receivables in batches, repeat issuance multiple times per year

Investor Benefits

  • Fixed returns — e.g. 5% annual yield over 2 years
  • Low risk via Senior Notes — first priority in repayment waterfall
  • Transparent structure — SPV isolates from Originator's business risk
  • Backed by stable receivables — predictable consumer loan repayments
ABS allows Monee to: Convert assets into capital · Separate risk legally · Retain long-term upside · Scale beyond a single funder relationship

Investor Portal — External Disclosure Layer

Why Does ABS Need an Investor Portal?

🔍

Transparency & Visibility

Investors commit large, long-term capital but cannot see internal ABS processes. The portal gives them a validated, read-only window into batch status and lifecycle events.

📉

Risk Monitoring

ABS performance is dynamic — cashflows fluctuate, risk fluctuates. Ongoing monitoring is crucial; the portal delivers timely updates without exposing internal operations.

⚖️

Trust & Compliance

Trust depends on timely, consistent reporting. The portal supports compliance, fairness, and audit traceability for both internal teams and regulators.

Why doesn't Channeling need a portal? Channeling banks get full transparency through direct system integrations — raw loan-level data pushed to their internal systems via SFTP/API daily. ABS external investors have no such feed, so the portal exists to give them equivalent visibility in a structured, controlled way.

Batch Lifecycle in the Investor Portal

Every batch lifecycle event in the Funding system automatically triggers a corresponding announcement and file upload in the Investor Portal:

Stage 1

Batch Creation

Maker creates batch creation ticket → Checker approves → batch status goes to INIT.
Portal: auto-announcement generated for Batch Create topic. Batch exists in system but hasn't undergone verification yet.

Stage 2

Batch Cutoff

System obtains pending sold report at cutoff D 12pm, applies RAC & traffic control, flags mismatches, calculates discounted selling price if discount model applies. Batch status: INIT → Batching/Matched → Pending_Sold → Sign_Confirm.
Portal: auto-announcement + Pending Sold Loan Tape uploaded. LocalCEO notified via announcement to complete review and signing offline.

Stage 3

Batch Sold

Maker creates sold ticket → Checker approves → batch status goes to SOLD. Finalizes the batch and officially sells it to SPV, triggering downstream settlement and payout allocation.
Portal: auto-announcement + Transferred Loan Tape uploaded.

Stage 4

Batch Revoke

Used to withdraw partial/entire batch — typically due to input errors or loan eligibility failure. Only available during Pending_Sold or Sign_Confirm status.
Portal: auto-announcement generated + Pending Sold Loan Tape re-uploaded.

Stage 5

Batch Update

Includes batch modification (editing target OS, cutoff date) or batch discard (cancel before cutoff). Only available up to T-2h before cutoff time.
Portal: auto-announcement + Pending Sold Loan Tape re-uploaded if applicable.

Stage 6

Clawback

Reclaiming of payouts on underperforming or potentially fraud-risky assets. Two modes: OSP + Accrued Int and Auto Pre-Restructure. System calculates clawback price if cutoff date is in the future and loan is valid.
Portal: Repurchased Loan Tape uploaded.

Scale: Monee currently runs 16 live ABS/ABL projects across 4 markets (TH, MY, ID, BR) — each with its own eligibility criteria, operation flow model, collection schedule, investor reporting cadence, and legal structure. The deal launches with a one-time "initial cut" — a defined pool of eligible receivables is legally transferred to the SPV, and the SPV pays Monee the purchase price. This is the foundation of the entire deal.

Phase 2: Revolving Period (Ongoing — e.g. 2 Years)

During the revolving period, Monee continuously sells new receivables to the SPV to replenish the portfolio. Each cycle repeats the same four-step workflow:

Step 1

Periodic Collection + Servicer Reporting

Monee collects repayments from borrowers and submits a servicer report to the SPV/Trustee detailing portfolio performance.

Step 2

Revolving Additional Receivable Sale

New eligible loans selected and Loan Tape submitted. SPV issues an Acknowledged Sales Notice confirming acceptance of new receivables.

Step 3

Collection Deposit / Set-Off

Collections reconciled and deposited to SPV. Net or gross transfer depending on the Ops Flow model in use for this project.

Step 4

Revolving Batch Sale Completion

SPV transfers purchase price to Monee. Batch confirmed sold. Cycle complete — repeat until revolving period ends.

Monthly Interest Payment Date — Waterfall Distribution: On each designated payment date, the SPV distributes collected cash strictly following the waterfall: ① Transaction costs & Trustee fees → ② Servicer fee (Monee) → ③ Senior Note interest & scheduled principal → ④ Junior Note interest & scheduled principal → ⑤ Residual/excess spread to Junior noteholder.

Ops Flow Models

Different projects use different operational flow models depending on capital efficiency requirements and funder agreement:

ModelCollection TransferReplenishment FrequencyTransfer ModeCapital Efficiency
Ops Flow A — ABS 3–4× / month 2–4× / month Non-Net Standard
Ops Flow B — ABS Net 4× / month 4× / month Net 100%
Ops Flow C — ABL 2× / month 1–2× / month Non-Net 90–95%

Phase 3: Amortization / Wind-Down (After Revolving Period Ends)

Once the revolving period ends, no new receivables are added. All borrower repayments flow through to investors per the amortization schedule:

What Happens

  • Revolving period closes — no new batches submitted to SPV
  • Existing pool continues generating repayments each month
  • SPV distributes repayments per waterfall — Senior first, then Junior
  • Pool gradually shrinks until all notes fully repaid and deal closes

Funding Team Role

  • Continue servicer reporting each period per transaction documents
  • Confirm collection transfers to SPV on schedule
  • Monitor portfolio KPIs — trigger breaches can accelerate payments even in wind-down
  • Handle clawback or repurchase obligations if assets breach representations

📊 Portfolio Criteria (PC)

Dynamic metrics for the portfolio as a whole — ensuring it remains diversified, low-risk, and yield-sufficient throughout the revolving period.

Concentration Limits

Avg disbursement ≤ THB 1,200 · Avg credit limit ≤ THB 60k · Top 500 users' OS ≤ 3.8% — prevents over-concentration in a few borrowers

Risk Limit

Liquidity & Yield

Allocation Strategy Proposal: Multi-Agent Optimization

The Core Challenge: A single loan may simultaneously satisfy eligibility rules of multiple funders. When one funder starts a new batch, it alters the remaining pool for others. Selecting "the best loans for Funder A" may harm B and C's ability to meet their targets. Local optimization is not enough.

The system evaluates every loan along two dimensions when a funder triggers a new batch:

Dimension 1: How much does THIS funder want this loan?

  • EC buffer (distance from eligibility cutoff)
  • Fit to tenor, risk, and concentration rules
  • Loan performance stability (DPD, Repayment Stability)

Dimension 2: How much do OTHER funders need this loan?

  • Don't occupy loans that B/C more critically need
  • From A's eligible pool, prefer loans: important for A, but not critical for B/C
  • Reserve high-value loans for funders who depend on them most

Funder Priority Ranking Model

When multiple funders are equally suitable for a loan, the system uses a Funder Priority Score to rank them:

FunderPriorityScore_j =
  w1 × FundingEfficiency_j // cost per dollar of funding
+ w2 × IRR_j // annualized return on transaction cash flows
+ w3 × VolumePotential_j // strategic scale opportunity with this funder

// Weights (w) are adjustable, manually configured to reflect business priorities
FactorWhat It Measures
Funding Efficiency All-in funding cost: effective interest rate + upfront fees + ongoing fees + operational costs. Lower = better.
IRR Annualized return generated from the transaction's cash flows. Captures effective interest rate, historical cost, cost per dollar funded.
Volume Potential Strategic importance of the funder — how much future business volume they represent to the platform.
Design principle: Weights are configurable — Funding team can adjust them to reflect current business priorities (e.g. prioritize lowest cost during tight liquidity, or prioritize volume potential when onboarding a strategic new funder).

FAQ — Common Questions

Q: Are we giving "good assets" to investors and keeping the bad ones?

No. It's about using the more stable, better-performing portion to attract external capital at lower cost, while the platform retains the tail-end risk as credit support. The platform retains the less certain portion as a risk buffer — reflecting both credit support AND a win-win structure. Investors want predictable cash flows (Senior Notes); Monee retains Junior Notes and shares in upside.

Q: How does the SPV "take the repayment risk"?

The SPV takes risk not by absorbing it directly, but by structuring, layering, and reserving to contain it:

  • Legal isolation via True Sale — receivables are legally transferred; SPV becomes the sole beneficiary (and risk holder) of those cash flows
  • Structured tranching — Senior Notes get paid first (least risk); Junior Notes take first losses
  • Reserve accounts — SPV holds cash reserves to absorb short-term shortfalls before they impact Senior Note holders

Q: Why do certain projects delay from original cutoff plans?

ABS projects go through a long pre-issuance lifecycle before system cutoff. Delays commonly occur at:

  • Due Diligence — legal/regulatory compliance review, credit enhancement analysis
  • Structuring & BRD — structuring decisions affect term sheet and then system requirements
  • Agreement & Regulatory Approval — timelines vary by jurisdiction
  • System Setup — Funding system development, DE data & portfolio selection, loan tape, reports

Appendix: ABS Project Lifecycle Overview

Stage 1

Project Initiation

Feasibility analysis (economic viability, risk, suitability for ABS). Preparation of indicative term sheet. Initial discussions on transaction structure, asset pool, key stakeholders.

Stage 2

Due Diligence & Term Sheet

Legal and regulatory compliance review. Credit enhancement and risk mitigation analysis. Group & company overview, acquisition strategy & metrics.

Stage 3

Structuring & Business Requirement Documentation

Raise BRD → Prepare PRD. Define transaction structure, pricing, eligibility criteria. Funding team begins system requirement definition.

Stage 4

Agreement & Regulatory Approval

Legal documentation finalized. Regulatory filings submitted. Rating agency engagement (if required). System and process setup begins.

Stage 5

Pre-Issuance

Investor roadshows (public projects). Funding system development, DE data & portfolio selection, loan tape, reports, pipeline development. ABS management workflow development.

Stage 6

Issuance (Launch)

Initial Cutoff — true sale of receivables to SPV. Senior Notes sold to investors. Funds flow back to Monee as consideration. ABS transaction is live.

Stage 7

Post-Closing Management

Ongoing pool replenishment via following cutoffs. Collection & reconciliation. Reporting to investors. Clawback/repurchase handling. System manages full servicer lifecycle.

Stage 8

Final Maturity & Wind-Down

All receivables collected and distributed. Senior Notes fully repaid. Junior Notes settled. SPV wound down. Batch status moved to Final.

Glossary

ABS

Asset-Backed Securities — fixed-income securities backed by a pool of loan receivables, issued by an SPV to capital markets investors.

ABL

Asset-Based Lending — a corporate loan where a bank lends against the value of pledged/assigned loan receivables. No securitization required.

SPV

Special Purpose Vehicle — a bankruptcy-remote shell company created solely to hold loan receivables and issue ABS. Has no other business activities.

True Sale

Legal transfer of receivable ownership from Monee to SPV. Removes assets from Monee's balance sheet. Critical for off-balance-sheet ABS treatment.

Originator

The entity (Monee) that originally created and owns the loan receivables being securitized.

Servicer

The entity (also Monee) that manages ongoing collection, customer service, and portfolio reporting after the true sale.

Tranche

A slice of the ABS structure with a specific risk/return profile. Senior (AAA) = lowest risk; Junior = higher risk and higher yield.

Repayment Waterfall

Contractual priority order for distributing SPV cash flows: fees → senior interest/principal → junior → residual excess spread.

Excess Spread

Yield surplus after paying all transaction costs and senior note coupons. Captured by Junior Note holders (Monee). Monee's upside in a well-performing pool.

Early Amortization ⚠️

Triggered by a covenant breach — revolving stops immediately, all collections used to repay investors early. Monee's biggest operational risk event.

Loan Tape

A detailed data file listing all loans in the asset pool with their attributes (balance, DPD, tenure, yield, etc.). Submitted to SPV at each cutoff.

AUP Audit

Agreed-Upon Procedures audit — independent external auditor validates Monee's compliance with transaction documents on a periodic basis.

Overcollateralization

Asset Cover Ratio > 100% — more receivables outstanding than notes outstanding. Provides a credit enhancement buffer protecting senior investors.

Revolving Period

The phase (e.g. 2 years) during which SPV buys new receivables to maintain pool size. Followed by the amortization/wind-down period.

DPD

Days Past Due — number of days a loan payment is overdue. Key credit quality indicator. Threshold limits defined in eligibility criteria (EC).

EIR

Effective Interest Rate — actual annualized yield on the loan portfolio, accounting for fees and timing. Key portfolio yield metric tracked against PC thresholds.

EC (Eligibility Criteria)

Static binary rules that determine whether an individual loan qualifies for inclusion in the ABS pool. Auto-applied by system at every cutoff.

PC (Portfolio Criteria)

Dynamic metrics applied to the portfolio as a whole — concentration, risk, liquidity, and yield limits that must be maintained throughout the revolving period.
